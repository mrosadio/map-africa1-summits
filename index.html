The Shifting Geography of Africa+1 Summits

In the context where African states are seeking to diversify their global partnerships, this interactive map offers insights into the deepening diplomatic ties between African countries and their traditional and emerging partners. It shows, from 1973 to 2024, the growing number of countries that have hosted Africa+1 summits, also called forums or international conferences. The term “Africa+1” refers to partnerships between African and non-African countries, bringing together presidents, ministers or high-level officials at summits to discuss different areas of cooperation, including trade, security, and development.

France held the first Africa+1 summit in 1973 and was the only host through 1992. Japan joined in 1993 and China in 2000. In 2008, Turkey and India hosted their first summits, followed by the United States (2014), Indonesia (2018), Russia (2019), the United Kingdom (2020), Saudi Arabia (2023), and South Korea and Italy (2024).

A country “hosts” a summit when it is the partner country that convenes it. The venue can be elsewhere: according to the APRI analysis, 12 of France’s 28 summits were held in African cities.

APRI conducted an extensive online search across multiple search engines to compile information and create a database on political summits where African presidents, ministers, or high-level officials met with their foreign counterparts. Although this search identified a significant number of summits, our publicly available database is not exhaustive. For instance, at this stage, the mapping exercise does not include the so-called ‘Africa business summits’. The map will be regularly updated with new information as it becomes available.
