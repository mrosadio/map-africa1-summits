The Shifting Geography of Africa+1 Summits

In the context where African states are seeking to diversify their global partnerships, this interactive map offers insights into the deepening diplomatic ties between African countries and their traditional and emerging partners. It shows, from 1973 to 2024, the growing number of countries that have hosted Africa+1 summits, also called as forums or international conferences. The term “Africa+1” refers to partnerships between African and non-African countries, bringing together presidents, ministers or high-level officials at summits to discuss different areas of cooperation, including trade, security, and development.

Diplomatic focus on Africa from global powers has shifted significantly since the 1970s. Initially, France was the sole country hosting Africa+1 summits, reflecting its close post-independence relationships with African countries from the 1970s to the end of the 1990s. From the early 2000s, countries in Asia, particularly China and Japan, began to invest more heavily in diplomatic and economic partnerships with African nations. By the 2010s, the landscape had diversified further, with Turkey, the United States, and Russia becoming prominent partners in Africa’s expanding international relations. More recently, Saudi Arabia and South Korea have begun to play an increasingly prominent role in this regard.

APRI conducted an extensive online search across multiple search engines to compile information and create a database on political summits where African presidents, ministers, or high-level officials met with their foreign counterparts. Although this search identified a significant number of summits, our publicly available database is not exhaustive. For instance, at this stage, the mapping exercise does not include the so-called ‘Africa business summits’. The map will be regularly updated with new information as it becomes available.
